Dafny's promise sounds like magic: write a specification, and the tool proves your code meets it — for every input, not just the ones you tested. The machinery behind that promise is a pipeline of three translations, each mechanical, each inspectable. Press the button and watch your program go through all of them.

Stage 2 — Boogie: everything made explicit

Dafny first translates into Boogie, a small verification language. Nothing is proved yet — but everything implicit becomes explicit: the heap is a real variable, each specification clause becomes an assert or assume, definite-assignment and termination get bookkeeping variables, and every statement keeps a breadcrumb (/input.dfy(line,col)) pointing back at your source. Stage 3 — One formula per proof obligation

Boogie then compresses each method into a single logical formula — the verification condition — in SMT-LIB, the standard language of solvers. The trick to reading it: the formula states that some path through your method violates a claim, and it is handed to the solver negated — inside (assert (not …)). Real variable names survive (x#0, y#0@1 — the @1 counts assignments), and ControlFlow encodes which path is taken:

Stage 4 — The verdict, by double negation

Z3 hunts for a way to satisfy that formula — that is, for a concrete execution that breaks your specification. The two possible answers:

unsat — no such execution exists. No input, no path, no heap can violate the claim. That is what “verified” means: not “tests passed”, but “a counterexample is mathematically impossible”.
sat — the solver found a witness, and Dafny turns its model into the counterexample you see in the demo's Problems tab.
